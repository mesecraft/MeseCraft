There are many crops you can grow, but some are more helpful than others. Perhaps the most useful are the grains: barley, cotton, oats, rice, rye and wheat.

-- Grains --

Seeds for these crops come from harvesting the grasses and wild plants found around the world. Harvesting a cultivated grain gives you both seeds and the crop, unlike most other crops, which serve as both seed and food.

Grains must also be processed before you can eat them. Grind barley, oats, rice, rye or wheat into flour, then bake it into bread, multigrain bread or rice bread.

Cotton is different from the other grains, and from every other crop: you don't eat it, you build and decorate with it. Cotton is a much more reliable source of wool than sheep, since four cotton make one wool. Wool is the start of all sorts of things: beds, chairs, carpets, ropes, curtains and more.

Take care with cotton and wheat, because they can be trampled. If a trampled crop is left unattended, the soil beneath it spoils and must be tilled again.
